# Model audit, 2026-11-03: findings before any fix

Scope: the `gamesim` engines (NFL play by play, NBA possession by possession with the player layer, NHL event clock),
their browser ports in Sim Lab (`simlab/index.html`), the exporter (`edge/simlab.py`), and the backtests.
Recorded before any fix. Phase 4 numbers that were still running when this was written are added under "Baseline
(before)" at the bottom. The fixes and before/after numbers are in `AUDIT_2026-11-03_after.md`.

Audit scripts: `scratchpad/audit/` (p1_js.js, p3_py.py, facts.py, corr.js, pl_check.js, bt_run.py, p4.py).

## CRITICAL

**C1. NBA endgame: when the trailing team is down 1 or 2 and the shot clock is off, it never gets the ball back.**
- *Code:* `gamesim/nba.py simulate()` and the browser `nbaPossGame`. `foul` requires a deficit of 3–8, so `hold` runs out the whole clock when the leader is up 1–2 with 24 s or less left.
- *Evidence (browser engine, PHI@DET, 20,000 runs per state):*

  | State | P(home wins) |
  |---|---|
  | Home +2, home ball, 20 s | 1.0000 |
  | Home +1, home ball, 15 s | 1.0000 |
  | Home +2, home ball, 30 s (shot clock on) | 0.8947 |
  | Home +2, away ball, 20 s | 0.8168 |

  100% is impossible: a trailing team fouls at once.
- *Impact:* every live and broadcast win probability in the last 24 s of a 1–2 point game. Pre-game effect is small.

## MAJOR

**M1. Live ratings drop last night's games.**
- *Cause:* `engines()` calls `ratings(G, today.isoformat())`, and ESPN timestamps are UTC. A 7 pm ET game is dated `T00:00Z` the next day, so `date < "2026-11-03"` excludes it.
- *Evidence:* 15 of 107 finished NBA games (14%) are dropped, including PHI's and DET's most recent games (CHI@PHI, DET@CHA on Nov 2), and 4 of 196 NHL games.

**M2. Backtest leakage from same-evening games.**
- *Cause:* backtests group games by UTC date (`g["date"][:10]`). An 8 pm ET game (UTC date = next day) is rated with games that tipped off at 7 pm ET the same evening and had not finished. Those are other teams' games, so the effect is small, but it is information that wasn't available. The NHL backtest also has the same flaw.

**M3. NBA pace shock double-counts possession variance.**
- *What happens:* the engine's own random possession lengths already give a per-team possession SD of 4.0. The observed game-pace SD after team effects is 4.09. On top of that the engine adds a lognormal pace shock with SD 5.04: the *raw* game-pace SD, which also includes team differences.
- *Evidence:*

  | | Per-team possession SD | Total-points SD |
  |---|---|---|
  | Simulated, with the shock | 6.2 | 22.1 |
  | Simulated, shock off | 4.0 | 19.4 |
  | Real, residual vs closing total (2025-26 / 2026-27) | – | 18.5 / 18.9 |

**M4. NBA margin distribution is too wide.**
- *Evidence:* margin SD 16.9, and still 17.05 with all shocks off. The real residual vs the closing spread is 14.1 (2025-26 and 2026-27). Overtime rate is 2.1% against 4.4–4.7% real. The extremes match: a 72-point margin and a 318 total appear in 50k runs of one game.
- *Cause:* possessions are independent, with no score effects (garbage time, a trailing team's urgency, a leader coasting). This is a design limitation, so it's a proposal below and is not changed here.

**M5. NFL scoring structure.**
- *Safeties: 0.196 per game against 0.044 real (2025), 4.5×.*
  - Yards pools use one field zone for the own 20 to the own goal line, so sacks and run losses typical of the own 20 are replayed at the own 3.
  - Kneel-downs also happen at the own 1, and each one is a safety (48 of 1,176 simulated safeties).
  - This is a sampling-boundary bug: the mirror image of the red-zone bias fixed earlier.
- *Key numbers:* the share of games with each final margin, for two identical teams vs real 2015–2025 games:

  | Final margin | Simulated | Real |
  |---|---|---|
  | 3 | 12.5% | 14.6% |
  | 4 | 6.8% | 4.7% |
  | 6 | 4.1% | 6.9% |
  | 7 | 11.0% | 8.7% |
  | 10 | 6.8% | 4.9% |

- *Contributors that are design choices (proposals, not fixed):*
  - Two-point tries come only from the 4th-quarter chart, at 0.11 per team-game against 0.235 real.
  - Touchdowns are 6% low and field goals 6% high (root cause not yet isolated).
  - Punts are 9% high.

**M6. NBA team strength ignores injuries.**
- *Evidence:* removing Cade Cunningham (33 min) moves DET's simulated points from 111.05 to 110.98. Only the player shares change.
- *Cause:* team ratings come from games he played. A market moves a line several points for an absence like this. Design limitation: proposal below.

**M7. NBA player distributions are too narrow, and the page doesn't say so.**
- *Evidence:* out of sample, 59 games and 1,164 player-games. 26.7% (points), 25.6% (rebounds), 27.4% (assists) and 25.0% (3s) of actual results land in the outer 10% tails, against 20% expected.
- *Skill at the page's default line, floor(mean) + 0.5:* none on points (Brier 0.2485 vs a base-rate coin flip of 0.2486) or rebounds (0.2485 vs 0.2488). Slight on assists (0.2427 vs 0.2480) and 3s (0.2289 vs 0.2395).
- *Honesty problem:* the page shows the 10–90% range and over/under percentages with no error statement.

**M8. NHL headline numbers use an assumed goalie with no stated basis.**
- *What happens:* the exporter takes each team's last starter (`R["starters"]`). The page names them only inside the factor table ("Projected goalies"), with no source or caveat next to the win probability.

**M9. The odds parsers crash on an "OFF" price** (`nhl.parse`, `data.espn_game`; NBA was fixed earlier). This aborts a season load or the exporter.
- *Status:* fixed during the audit, because it blocked loading the 2025-26 NHL season (1,313 games). Disclosed here; nothing else was changed before this report.

## MINOR

- **m1. RNG reseeding doesn't reset the normal-draw cache.** `seed()` in the browser worker leaves the Box–Muller spare, so the same seed gives a different first normal draw depending on history (−1.6998 vs −0.1309 in the test). This breaks common random numbers between factor-table scenarios (extra noise, no bias).
- **m2. Player minutes can fall short of 240.** The per-player cap at 48 minutes is applied after scaling, so a team's total in one simulation can come up to 3.1 minutes short.
- **m3. Endless-overtime tie-break.** After 8 overtimes a tie counts as a home win (never observed).
- **m4. NHL distribution slightly too dispersed** (no score effects), compared with real 2025-26 / 2026-27:

  | | Simulated | Real |
  |---|---|---|
  | Total-goals SD | 2.40 | 2.30 / 2.14 |
  | P(total ≥ 10) | 9.3% | 7.1% / 5.6% |
  | Overtime rate | 20.2% | 24.8% / 21.9% |
  | One-goal games | 38.2% | 43.2% / 39.8% |

- **m5. NFL margin and total SD 4–6% narrow:** 12.2 vs a 12.7 real residual, and 12.4 vs 13.2.
- **m6. The player backtest treats actual DNPs as known** (disclosed on the page).
- **m7. NHL empty-net rates overlap the backtest.** The rates come from the first 120 games of the season, which overlap the current-season backtest (tiny leakage). The 2025-26 audit backtest uses 2024-25 rates.
- **m8. NBA home edge is fragile early in the season.** It is calibrated to the in-season home margin only, with a standard error of about 1.4 points at 90 games and about 3 at 20. NHL already uses last season's for this reason.
- **m9. Inputs and their sources:**
  - NHL home ice is last season's (documented).
  - NFL starters come from a dated depth-chart snapshot (labelled).
  - Injuries carry the pull time.

## Proposals (not applied: need approval)

1. **NBA score effects.** Make possession efficiency and pace depend on the score margin and time, calibrated so the margin residual SD matches about 14.1 and overtime about 4.5% (fixes M4, part of m4 for NHL).
2. **NBA injuries in team strength.** Adjust team ratings by the on/off or minutes-weighted value of absent players (M6).
3. **NFL two-point decisions.** Fit them from data for all quarters, not the 4th-quarter chart only, and isolate the touchdown/field-goal mix (M5).
4. **Player game-to-game variance.** Add a per-player per-game volume and efficiency shock, calibrated so the PIT tails come back to about 20% (M7).
5. **NBA home edge.** Shrink the in-season home margin toward last season's (m8).
